Section by section

What each part of the page does

Every screen below is the live site, captured as it is today.

The opening screen

The property's best photographs cross-fade slowly behind the name, with a booking button and a call button placed before any scrolling is needed. Underneath sits an availability bar: dates, number of guests and the occasion.

Filling it in and pressing Check Availability opens WhatsApp with the enquiry already written out.

Why it matters A guest who arrives on a phone can send a real enquiry in about ten seconds, without scrolling, typing an address, or finding a form.

Ways to stay

Each way of booking the property gets a card: a photograph, a short description, the facilities included, and its own Enquire Now button that opens WhatsApp.

Cards are not fixed in the design — they are a list in one settings file. Three today, five next season, and the layout arranges itself.

Why it matters Each card is a separate enquiry route, so the owner knows what the guest was looking at before the conversation starts.

Rates, stated plainly

Prices sit in the open with everything they include listed underneath, and one plan can be marked as the recommended one. Check-in and check-out times are written once in the settings and appear on every card and in the FAQ answer.

Why it matters Published prices filter out the enquiries that were never going to book, and a single source for the timings means they can never contradict each other.

The gallery

Photographs are laid out as a mosaic — some large, some small — rather than identical squares, and grouped into tabs that come from the photo folders themselves. Add a folder of spa photos and a Spa tab appears on its own.

Tapping any photo opens it full-screen with its description, a counter, and arrow-key navigation.

Why it matters Photographs sell a property. This is the part of the page guests spend longest on, so it takes every photo the property has without anyone resizing or renaming a file.

Full-screen viewer

The viewer dims the page, shows the photo's description and its position in the set, and can be driven with arrow keys or swipes. Pressing Escape closes it and returns focus to the photo that was opened.

Why it matters Guests browse a gallery the way they browse a phone camera roll. This behaves the way they already expect, including for keyboard and screen-reader users.

Amenities and questions

Amenities are grouped — the property, events, overnight stays, languages spoken — so a guest scanning for one thing finds it. The FAQ below folds open one answer at a time.

Why it matters Both sections are the phone calls the owner takes every week, written down once. Google also reads the FAQ and can show those answers directly in search results.

Finding the place

The map does not load until a visitor asks for it. Until then they see a photograph of the surroundings and a Load map button; the address, a Get Directions link, a call button and a WhatsApp button are all there without it.

Why it matters Nobody is handed to Google before they choose to be, and the page stays fast for the many visitors who only wanted the phone number.

The enquiry form

Name, phone, dates, how many adults and children, what kind of stay, and a message. It checks the phone number, refuses a check-out before check-in, and will not offer dates in the past.

On submit it opens WhatsApp with the whole enquiry composed as a message. There is no inbox to watch and no form service to pay for.

Why it matters Enquiries arrive where the owner already is. The reply is a WhatsApp reply — no missed email, no spam folder, no login.

On a phone

What the owner gets

Six things this does for the business

A website is only worth building if it changes something. Here is what changes.

Direct bookings

No commission on any enquiry

Every button on the page opens a WhatsApp conversation with the owner. Listing portals typically keep 15–25% of a booking; enquiries that arrive here keep all of it.

Running cost

Nothing to pay each month

The site is plain files. It runs on free static hosting, needs no database, no plugin licences and no form service. Only the domain name is renewed each year.

Speed

Loads on a weak 4G signal

The whole page — markup, styling and behaviour — is about 50 KB compressed. Photographs are cut into four sizes so a phone downloads a phone-sized copy, not the original.

Search

Built to be found

The page tells Google it is a resort, where it is, what it costs and what the FAQ answers are — in the structured format Google reads. That is what earns the expanded search result.

Upkeep

Staff can update it

New photos are dropped into a folder — any filename, any number. Text, prices and the phone number live in one clearly marked file. No admin panel to learn or lose the password to.

Security

Nothing to break into

No login, no database, no plugins going out of date. The most common way small business sites get hacked simply does not exist here.

Keeping it current

Adding this season's photos takes three steps

This is the whole process. There is no fourth step and no software to open.

1

Drop the photos in

Put them in the folder for that part of the site — pool, rooms, food. Filenames do not matter. Five photos shows five; add three more and it shows eight.

2

Push the change

The host picks it up and rebuilds the fast copies of every new photo by itself — four sizes each, in the modern format, with the dimensions recorded.

3

It is live

The gallery, its filter tabs and every section that uses those photos rearrange themselves around what is now there. Nothing to configure.

Words, prices, the phone number and the address are the same idea: one settings file, plainly commented. The WhatsApp number is written in one place and every button on the page follows it — so it can never be right in one spot and wrong in another.

Built to hold

What happens when something goes wrong

Small business sites usually fail quietly — a broken image here, a blank section there. Each of these was designed for rather than hoped about.

  • A visitor blocks JavaScript. They still get the name, phone number, WhatsApp link and address, not an empty page.
  • A photo fails to load. The page quietly retries with the untouched original before giving up.
  • A section has no content yet. It hides itself instead of leaving a heading over blank space.
  • The photo build fails. The last good version is already committed and gets served — a failed build cannot take the site down.
  • A guest asks for reduced motion. The cross-fades and reveals switch off; the content is unaffected.
  • Someone visits a wrong address. A proper 404 page in the site's own design points them back.

One build, many properties

The same site, rebranded for the next client in a day

Because every word, price, colour and photograph is a setting rather than something typed into the layout, the whole site can be pointed at a different property without touching the design work.

That has already been done once. A second version of this site is fully rebranded for Bade Farms, a private five-bedroom villa on two acres in Kolhapur — new name, new colours, new copy, new rates, new structure for the stay options (a whole villa, pool parties, weddings) and a new address and WhatsApp number.

It also ships with an honest record of what is verified and what is not: the phone number came from the property's own Instagram, the rate from wedding directories. Invented reviews and self-published star ratings were left out on purpose — Google's own policy forbids a business marking up reviews of itself.
